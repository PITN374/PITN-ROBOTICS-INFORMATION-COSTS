EXECUTIVE SUMMARY\
\-------------------------------------------------------------------------------\
\
Robotics manufacturers depend on information about components, assemblies,\
suppliers, technical specifications, revisions, bills of materials (BOMs), receiving,\
installation, maintenance, and lifecycle history. The information may already exist,\
but locating it and reliably connecting it across separate systems can require\
repeated manual work.\
\
POWER IN THE NUMBERS (PITN) addresses this information-friction problem through\
persistent digital identity, blockchain/Web3 namespace classification, and\
relationships that connect physical components to the information that already\
describes them. PITN is designed as an identity and relationship layer that\
complements manufacturers' existing ERP, PLM, MES, CAD, PDM, QMS, warehouse,\
inventory, and maintenance systems—not a replacement for those systems.\
\
The economic premise is testable: when component information can be found and\
related more reliably, some repetitive information work may take less time, require\
less re-entry or reconciliation, and result in fewer avoidable errors. The savings\
must be measured in a manufacturer's actual workflow. Architecture alone does not\
prove time savings, and projected savings are not guaranteed results.\
\
This study uses two separate illustrative models:\
\
1\. An example annual manufacturing software-stack budget of $257,000 across eight\
&#x20;  categories. This is a planning scenario assembled from category-level estimates,\
&#x20;  not a published industry average, audited spend, or universal manufacturer cost.\
2\. A hypothetical $2,500,000 annual information-task burden with modeled reductions\
&#x20;  totaling $572,500, or 22.9%. These assumptions are taken from PITN's\
&#x20;  Manufacturer Implementation and Information Savings Blueprint. They are a\
&#x20;  constructed reference scenario, not measured PITN customer results.\
\
The two models are compared only to illustrate economic scale. The $572,500\
modeled information savings do not constitute proof that PITN can pay for or replace\
software licenses. A manufacturer-specific study must measure task volumes, time\
before and after implementation, labor rates, quality of outcomes, implementation\
costs, ongoing operating costs, and net benefits.\
\
The central PITN operating principle is:\
\
CONNECT THE PHYSICAL COMPONENT TO THE INFORMATION THAT ALREADY DESCRIBES IT.\
\
The economic proposition is to reduce the effort required to identify, reconcile,\
retrieve, and maintain component information across the manufacturing information\
environment.\
\
\-------------------------------------------------------------------------------\
1\. THE MANUFACTURING INFORMATION PROBLEM\
\-------------------------------------------------------------------------------\
\
Robotics manufacturers work with a large and expanding population of physical parts,\
components, assemblies, subsystems, and finished robot systems.\
\
A humanoid robot may incorporate, among other things:\
\
\- Structural components, frames, links, housings, and mechanical assemblies.\
\- Motors, actuators, reducers, gears, robotic joints, and motion components.\
\- Bearings, fasteners, connectors, pins, and cable harnesses.\
\- Encoders, force sensors, torque sensors, IMUs, and other sensing devices.\
\- Controllers, circuit boards, embedded electronics, and power-conversion equipment.\
\- Battery cells, battery modules, power-distribution assemblies, and charging systems.\
\- Cameras, vision sensors, depth sensors, and integrated perception modules.\
\- End effectors, grippers, fingers, and tool-changing mechanisms.\
\
Each component or assembly may be represented by multiple identifiers and records,\
including:\
\
\- Manufacturer Part Number (MPN).\
\- Supplier Stock Keeping Unit (SKU).\
\- Internal manufacturer part number.\
\- Engineering part number.\
\- Revision or configuration identifier.\
\- Lot or batch number.\
\- Serial number.\
\- Purchase order and receiving reference.\
\- Technical specification.\
\- CAD model and engineering drawing.\
\- Certification, inspection, or test record.\
\- Supplier documentation.\
\- BOM position and parent assembly.\
\- Installation location.\
\- Maintenance, replacement, and lifecycle history.\
\
These records can exist in separate databases, software systems, documents, supplier\
portals, spreadsheets, and other information sources. The issue is not necessarily\
that information is missing. It is the effort required to establish which records\
relate to the same component, which are current, which are authoritative, and how\
the component fits into a larger assembly or lifecycle.\
\
PITN focuses on this connecting and retrieval burden.\
\
\-------------------------------------------------------------------------------\
2\. INFORMATION WORK THAT CREATES COST\
\-------------------------------------------------------------------------------\
\
Information-related effort may be distributed across engineering, procurement,\
sourcing, production, receiving, inventory, quality, service, and maintenance.\
It may be embedded in payroll and operating costs rather than recorded as a single\
software or "information expense."\
\
Examples of tasks include:\
\
A. Component identification\
&#x20;  \- Finding a component from an MPN, SKU, internal number, label, or description.\
&#x20;  \- Matching supplier identifiers to internal engineering or ERP records.\
&#x20;  \- Confirming whether multiple identifiers refer to one item or different items.\
\
B. Technical information retrieval\
&#x20;  \- Locating datasheets, drawings, CAD files, specifications, certifications, or\
&#x20;    supplier documents.\
&#x20;  \- Confirming the source and date of information.\
&#x20;  \- Determining whether a document applies to the current product revision.\
\
C. BOM reconciliation\
&#x20;  \- Matching part records to BOM positions.\
&#x20;  \- Confirming parent assemblies, quantities, configurations, and revisions.\
&#x20;  \- Finding duplicate or conflicting BOM entries.\
\
D. Revision and configuration research\
&#x20;  \- Determining the correct revision or configuration.\
&#x20;  \- Finding which assemblies or robots are affected by an engineering change.\
&#x20;  \- Locating superseded information while preserving history.\
\
E. Supplier and procurement research\
&#x20;  \- Matching supplier SKU and manufacturer identifiers.\
&#x20;  \- Retrieving authorized supplier information, quotations, lead times, and\
&#x20;    purchasing references.\
&#x20;  \- Resolving missing or conflicting supplier data.\
\
F. Replacement and compatibility research\
&#x20;  \- Identifying potential replacements or alternatives.\
&#x20;  \- Locating relevant interface, specification, and engineering records.\
&#x20;  \- Confirming whether an alternative is actually approved or validated.\
&#x20;  NOTE: A matching name, namespace, or identity does not prove compatibility.\
&#x20;      Engineering or manufacturer validation is required.\
\
G. Installed-component and lifecycle retrieval\
&#x20;  \- Identifying which component or serial instance is installed in a robot or\
&#x20;    subsystem.\
&#x20;  \- Locating receiving, installation, maintenance, replacement, and disposition\
&#x20;    records.\
&#x20;  \- Connecting removed or recovered components to their relevant history.\
\
These tasks can involve repeated searching, manual data entry, record matching,\
clarification, and verification. PITN's proposed value is to reduce the amount of\
repeat work required to make these connections.\
\

3\. PITN: IDENTITY AND INFORMATION-CONNECTION LAYER\
\-------------------------------------------------------------------------------\
\
PITN is the umbrella infrastructure supporting six complementary initiatives:\
\
\- POWER IN THE NUMBERS (PITN): shared identity, namespace, infrastructure, and\
&#x20; traceability foundation.\
\- ROBOPARTS.ai: component identification, part information, BOM relationships,\
&#x20; sourcing references, and lifecycle information.\
\- ROBOTRONIC.ai: robotics integration, system interoperability, and\
&#x20; simulation-oriented technical specifications.\
\- ROBOTRAITS.ai: representation of robotic traits, capabilities, and behavioral\
&#x20; configurations.\
\- ROBOAGENTIC.ai: agent identity, authorization, orchestration, provenance, and\
&#x20; revocation concepts.\
\- ROBOASI.ai: AI, information, and knowledge infrastructure.\
\- ROBORECYCLE.ai: component recovery, refurbishment, reuse, and lifecycle recovery.\
\
PITN's manufacturer-facing information-cost case begins with persistent identity\
and ROBOPARTS.ai component information.\
\
Architecture chain:\
Physical Components -> Digital Identity -> Capabilities -> Robots -> Intelligence\
-> Tasks -> Applications\
\
PITN's identity layer is intended to connect a physical component to its related\
information using persistent identity references, classification, and relationships.\
\
The namespace architecture is blockchain/Web3-based. Namespace, registered\
digital name, component identity record, and physical component are distinct:\
\
1\. Namespace: classification and naming domain.\
2\. Registered digital name: a name registered within a namespace under applicable\
&#x20;  provider rules.\
3\. Component identity record: structured information connecting a product\
&#x20;  definition or physical instance to identifiers, references, and relationships.\
4\. Physical component: the actual manufactured, purchased, received, installed,\
&#x20;  or serviced item.\
\
A namespace is not by itself a complete component record, proof of physical\
authenticity, technical verification, certification, or compatibility.\
\
PITN does not require a manufacturer to abandon existing MPNs, SKUs, internal\
part numbers, serial numbers, or supplier identifiers. Original identifiers remain\
in place and are associated with a persistent identity reference. Source systems\
remain authoritative for the records and processes they control.\
\
The intended information path is:\
\
PHYSICAL COMPONENT\
&#x20;       |\
&#x20;       v\
PITN PERSISTENT IDENTITY / NAMESPACE CLASSIFICATION\
&#x20;       |\
&#x20;       v\
ASSOCIATED IDENTIFIERS AND RELATIONSHIPS\
&#x20;       |\
&#x20;       v\
AUTHORIZED REFERENCES TO TECHNICAL, BOM, SUPPLIER,\
ENGINEERING, QUALITY, INSTALLATION, AND LIFECYCLE RECORDS\
&#x20;       |\
&#x20;       v\
EXISTING MANUFACTURER SYSTEMS\
ERP / PLM / MES / CAD / PDM / QMS / WMS / CMMS / SUPPLIER SYSTEMS\
\
PITN connects records and references; it does not automatically copy, validate,\
replace, or take authority over the source records.\

4\. WHY IDENTITY CONNECTIONS MAY REDUCE INFORMATION COST\
\-------------------------------------------------------------------------------\
\
The cost-reduction hypothesis is based on five mechanisms. Each mechanism is\
measurable and should be tested rather than assumed.\
\
MECHANISM 1 — LESS SEARCH AND RE-SEARCH\
\
A persistent identity reference can act as a repeatable starting point for finding\
authorized associated information. If the employee no longer has to independently\
search multiple systems and documents to re-establish the same relationships,\
retrieval time may decrease.\
\
Potential measures:\
\- Average and median time to locate component records.\
\- Number of systems or searches required per task.\
\- Repeat searches for the same component.\
\- Successful retrieval rate.\
\
MECHANISM 2 — LESS IDENTIFIER RECONCILIATION\
\
The identity record can associate the MPN, supplier SKU, internal part number,\
engineering number, and other identifiers while preserving each identifier and its\
source. This may reduce time spent manually matching records.\
\
Potential measures:\
\- Time to match identifiers.\
\- Number of manual lookups and cross-checks.\
\- Identifier mismatch or unresolved-match rate.\
\- Duplicate investigations.\
\
MECHANISM 3 — FASTER BOM AND ASSEMBLY NAVIGATION\
\
Relationships between component identity references and BOM positions or parent\
assemblies can help a user navigate from a component to its context and from an\
assembly to its constituent components.\
\
Potential measures:\
\- BOM reconciliation time.\
\- Time to identify parent assembly or position.\
\- Number of manual searches to trace component relationships.\
\- Relationship accuracy against manufacturer-approved BOMs.\
\
MECHANISM 4 — LESS REPEATED INFORMATION ENTRY AND CLARIFICATION\
\
When approved references and identifiers are accessible, employees may need less\
repeated data entry or clarification between departments and suppliers.\
\
Potential measures:\
\- Duplicate entry count.\
\- Supplier clarification requests attributable to identifier/data ambiguity.\
\- Repeated internal requests for already-existing information.\
\- Time spent resolving incomplete or conflicting records.\
\
MECHANISM 5 — IMPROVED LIFECYCLE INFORMATION RETRIEVAL\
\
Persistent identity can help connect a component product definition with physical\
instances and applicable receiving, installation, service, replacement, and\
disposition records. The manufacturer must define and validate the relationships.\
\
Potential measures:\
\- Time to identify installed component or instance.\
\- Time to retrieve maintenance or replacement information.\
\- Time to trace a component to a subsystem or robot.\
\- Correctness and completeness of retrieved lifecycle references.\
\
The benefits are not guaranteed. If the manufacturer's information is already\
well-integrated, records are clean, and retrieval is efficient, PITN may deliver\
limited incremental savings for that workflow. The study must capture both positive\
and negative results.\

7\. CATEGORY-LEVEL EXAMPLES: HOW THE SAVINGS COULD OCCUR\
\-------------------------------------------------------------------------------\
\
EXAMPLE A — ENGINEERING IDENTIFICATION AND BOM RECONCILIATION\
\
Current workflow example:\
An engineer receives an internal part number, supplier SKU, or MPN. The engineer\
searches ERP, PLM/PDM, supplier documentation, CAD references, and BOM records\
to confirm the item and the correct revision. If records cannot be matched\
confidently, the engineer asks procurement or a supplier to clarify.\
\
PITN-enabled workflow hypothesis:\
The component identity provides a repeatable reference connecting the approved\
identifiers and authorized references to relevant BOM, technical, and revision\
records. The engineer starts from the identity reference rather than repeatedly\
rebuilding the association from disparate identifiers.\
\
Potential saving:\
Reduced time spent locating and matching component information and reconciling\
BOM records.\
\
Example calculation (illustrative only):\
\- 2,000 information tasks per year.\
\- Baseline: 18 minutes per task.\
\- PITN-enabled: 10 minutes per task.\
\- Time saved: 8 minutes per task.\
\- Annual time saved: 2,000 x 8 / 60 = 266.67 hours.\
\- Loaded labor rate: $60/hour.\
\- Illustrative annual labor savings: 266.67 x $60 = $16,000.\
\
This example is not the same as the $150,000 modeled category savings in the\
reference scenario. It shows how a manufacturer can calculate a task-level result\
from its own volume and measured times. The two models should not be added\
together unless the task populations are proven distinct.\
\
EXAMPLE B — SUPPLIER INFORMATION AND SOURCING RESEARCH\
\
Current workflow example:\
Procurement must connect a supplier's SKU or catalog record to the manufacturer's\
internal part record, technical specification, approved supplier status, and\
existing sourcing information. This can require searches, supplier messages,\
and manual cross-reference work.\
\
PITN-enabled workflow hypothesis:\
The identity record connects the original identifiers and authorized supplier\
references. Procurement can use the identity as a starting point for retrieving\
the relevant information, while source authority and supplier validation remain\
in place.\
\
Potential saving:\
Reduced repeat searches and time spent reconciling supplier identifiers and\
already-available sourcing information.\
\
Example calculation (illustrative only):\
\- 1,500 supplier information tasks annually.\
\- Baseline: 20 minutes each.\
\- PITN-enabled: 12 minutes each.\
\- Time saved: 8 minutes each.\
\- Annual hours saved: 200 hours.\
\- Loaded labor rate: $55/hour.\
\- Illustrative annual savings: $11,000.\
\
PITN does not automatically establish supplier approval, product availability,\
price, lead time, or technical equivalence.\
\
EXAMPLE C — DUPLICATE RECORDS AND DATA CLEANUP\
\
Current workflow example:\
Different teams create records for a part under different names or identifiers.\
Employees investigate whether the records are duplicates, distinct revisions,\
different configurations, or separate physical instances. Incorrect merging can\
cause errors, so records must be carefully reviewed.\
\
PITN-enabled workflow hypothesis:\
Persistent identity and explicit mappings may help expose related records and\
preserve their original identifiers, reducing the effort needed to locate and\
review potential duplicates.\
\
Potential saving:\
Less time spent searching for duplicate records and investigating identifier\
conflicts.\
\
Example calculation (illustrative only):\
\- 600 duplicate or conflict investigations annually.\
\- Baseline: 30 minutes each.\
\- PITN-enabled: 18 minutes each.\
\- Time saved: 12 minutes each.\
\- Annual hours saved: 600 x 12 / 60 = 120 hours.\
\- Loaded labor rate: $50/hour.\
\- Illustrative annual savings: 120 x $50 = $6,000.\
\
PITN does not automatically decide that two similar records are the same part.\
A qualified data owner must approve identity matches and preserve distinctions.\
\

11\. SAVINGS FORMULA AND MANUFACTURER ROI\
\-------------------------------------------------------------------------------\
\
Use the following formula for each workflow:\
\
Annual task labor cost =\
Annual task volume x Average task minutes / 60 x Loaded hourly labor cost\
\
Annual gross labor savings =\
Baseline annual task labor cost - Post-implementation annual task labor cost\
\
Annual net benefit =\
Gross labor savings - Incremental recurring PITN operating costs\
\
Net implementation-period benefit =\
Gross savings over the evaluation period - Initial implementation costs -\
Operating costs over the evaluation period\
\
Simple payback period (months) =\
Initial implementation costs / Average monthly net benefit\
\
Only use payback calculations when the average monthly net benefit is positive.\
If measured benefits do not exceed implementation and recurring costs, the pilot\
does not establish a positive payback.\
\
Include all relevant incremental costs, such as integration, mapping, configuration,\
training, licensing, support, infrastructure, maintenance, security review, and\
internal implementation labor.\
\
For a manufacturer-specific calculation, measure these inputs:\
\
\- Tasks per year by workflow and department.\
\- Baseline time per task.\
\- Post-implementation time per task.\
\- Loaded labor rate by employee role.\
\- Task accuracy and successful completion.\
\- Errors, exceptions, and rework.\
\- Implementation costs.\
\- Ongoing operating costs.\
\- Any additional savings claimed, measured separately.\
\
Do not count the same time reduction twice. Do not automatically monetize quality,\
downtime, inventory, scrap, throughput, or warranty improvements without evidence.\

12\. PILOT: A CONTROLLED WAY TO TEST THE PITN SAVINGS HYPOTHESIS\
\-------------------------------------------------------------------------------\
\
A manufacturer can evaluate PITN on a narrow, representative component family\
before considering broader deployment.\
\
Recommended pilot approach:\
\
STEP 1 — Define the scope\
Select a robot platform, subsystem, or component family (for example, actuators,\
reducers, encoders, sensors, connectors, or structural parts). Identify the\
participating departments and information systems.\
\
STEP 2 — Capture the baseline\
Before PITN-enabled workflow use, record task volumes, task completion times,\
search steps, errors, unsuccessful retrievals, and the loaded labor rates that will\
be used in the financial model.\
\
STEP 3 — Classify and map identifiers\
Select the applicable PITN namespace classification. Map existing MPNs, SKUs,\
internal part numbers, revisions, lot/serial references, and source records.\
Preserve the original identifiers and source authority.\
\
STEP 4 — Establish identity and relationships\
Create or associate persistent identity references. Connect manufacturer-approved\
records, BOM positions, parent assemblies, supplier references, technical\
documents, and lifecycle references where authorized and applicable.\
\
STEP 5 — Configure the workflow\
Use the approved application or manufacturer-approved integration method.\
The implementation scope must specify which systems, data, records, interfaces,\
permissions, and functions are actually included. Do not assume an unconfigured\
system integration is already available.\
\
STEP 6 — Train and test\
Use a controlled test set. Include typical cases, exceptions, duplicate records,\
revision conflicts, incomplete records, and unsuccessful retrieval attempts.\
Keep validation criteria consistent.\
\
STEP 7 — Measure post-implementation performance\
Repeat comparable tasks. Record time, success, correctness, errors, exceptions,\
and repeated work. Do not measure speed alone; a faster but incorrect retrieval\
is not a saving.\
\
STEP 8 — Calculate gross and net savings\
Use observed time differences and task volumes. Deduct implementation and\
ongoing costs. Separate labor savings from other benefits and avoid double\
counting.\
\
STEP 9 — Review and decide\
Document the results, limitations, data quality issues, and acceptance criteria.\
Expand only if the manufacturer determines that results justify doing so.\

17\. THE MANUFACTURER'S DECISION\

\
The relevant question for a robotics manufacturer is not whether PITN should replace\

\
The relevant questions are:\
\

&#x20; information that already exists?\

18\. CONCLUSION\
\-------------------------------------------------------------------------------\
\
Robotics manufacturers are not only producing physical components and robot\
assemblies. They are continually creating, maintaining, reconciling, and\
retrieving information about those components throughout engineering, sourcing,\
manufacturing, quality, inventory, installation, service, and lifecycle operations.\
\
As robotics supply chains expand and humanoid systems incorporate more components,\
suppliers, assemblies, configurations, revisions, and physical instances, the\
ability to connect component identity to existing information becomes increasingly\
important.\
\
PITN's persistent identity and blockchain/Web3 namespace architecture is designed\
to address this connection problem. By providing classification, persistent\
identity references, and relationships between component records, PITN seeks to\
reduce the effort required to identify, reconcile, retrieve, and maintain\
component information while preserving existing system authority.\
\
The financial case is a hypothesis that can be tested. The illustrative software\
stack shows the scale of a manufacturer's existing systems investment. The\
information-cost model shows how task-level reductions can be translated into\
annual labor savings. The Blueprint's $2.5 million baseline and $572,500\
modeled savings illustrate a potential outcome, but they are not measured results.\
\
The proof must come from a manufacturer-specific baseline, a controlled\
PITN-enabled pilot, comparable task measurements, validated results, and a\
financial calculation that deducts implementation and operating costs.\
\
The intended result is not another isolated database. It is a persistent identity\
and information-connection foundation that reduces the work required to connect\
physical robotics components to the information that already describes them.\
