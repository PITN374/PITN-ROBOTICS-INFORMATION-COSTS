1. PURPOSE AND RELATIONSHIP TO EXISTING PITN RESEARCH
======================================================================

This document provides a focused economic analysis of the potential costs and financial benefits associated with adopting the POWER IN THE NUMBERS (PITN) persistent component identity architecture in robotics manufacturing.

It supplements the existing PITN research publications and technical architecture documents.

It does not repeat the existing explanations of the PITN architecture, the 34-namespace classification, component identity relationships, or the ROBOPARTS.ai application.

Instead, it evaluates five economic questions:

1. What does persistent blockchain/Web3 identity acquisition cost at scale?
2. How do registration acquisition costs compare with existing manufacturing information expenditures?
3. What potential labor savings could result from reducing repetitive information work?
4. What are the complete costs of adopting and operating PITN?
5. What economic and commercial opportunities could arise from manufacturer deployment and downstream licensing?

The analysis separates registration expenditure, potential registration-owner returns, existing software expenditure, modeled labor savings, implementation costs, and commercial licensing revenue.

These are distinct financial categories and must not be combined or presented as interchangeable savings.

All financial scenarios are illustrative unless identified as PITN-reported historical information or an established contractual arrangement.

======================================================================

The supplied PITN registration material describes:

- An illustrative listed registration price of $5.00.
- Promotional discounts that may reduce the actual purchase price.
- A described 50/50 revenue-sharing arrangement under which the namespace owner receives 50% of the eligible actual purchase price.
- A PITN-reported historical average purchase price of approximately $1.75 per registration.

The $1.75 figure is a PITN-reported historical average from the supplied material. It is not an independently verified market-wide blockchain/Web3 registration average.

2.2 ECONOMIC CALCULATION

Consolidated Net Economic Cost =

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION:

The actual purchaser pays the applicable registration price.

The owner return is a separate economic receipt under the described revenue-sharing arrangement.

A manufacturer cannot automatically deduct the owner return from its registration expenditure unless it is contractually entitled to receive that return.

For an eligible namespace owner receiving the described 50% return, the consolidated economic cost may be modeled at 50% of the eligible purchase price.

For a manufacturer without that entitlement, the applicable registration expenditure remains the full actual purchase price.

Provider terms, promotional eligibility, minimums, fees, taxes, transaction costs, renewals, and payment conditions must be verified before any financial commitment.

The 50% arrangement must not be represented as guaranteed for every registration.

3.2 REGISTRATION GRANULARITY AND COST CONTROL

A manufacturer should not automatically assume that every physical component requires an individual paid namespace registration.

A robotics manufacturer may use:

- One identity for a shared product definition.
- Separate records for different engineering revisions.
- Manufacturer-controlled serial numbers for individual physical units.
- Registered identities for individual physical instances where justified.
- Separate identities for assemblies or subsystems.

For example, a manufacturer producing 500 humanoid robots may use thousands of repeated actuators belonging to a smaller number of product definitions.

The appropriate registration quantity depends on the approved identity architecture and operational requirements.

Registration granularity is therefore a significant cost-control decision.

The manufacturer should establish the number of identities actually requiring registration before calculating acquisition expenditure.

The $257,000 figure represents an illustrative annual manufacturing software expenditure scenario.

It is not a verified industry average, vendor quotation, or PITN savings claim.

Actual expenditures depend on:

- Manufacturer size.
- Number of employees and users.
- Software modules.
- Licensing arrangements.
- Cloud or on-premises deployment.
- Implementation and integration.
- Support contracts.
- Enterprise purchasing agreements.

Some software categories overlap or are bundled.

For example, BOM and inventory functionality may be included in ERP, PDM may be included in CAD or PLM, and quality or maintenance functions may be included in other platforms.

PITN is designed to complement these systems.

It is not presented as a replacement for them.

PITN does not automatically eliminate or reduce existing software licensing invoices.

Any software expenditure reduction would require a separate purchasing, consolidation, or contractual decision by the manufacturer.

The $257,000 baseline is included to demonstrate the scale of existing manufacturing information infrastructure expenditure against which a manufacturer can evaluate additional identity infrastructure costs.

11. COMMERCIAL LICENSING AND DOWNSTREAM REVENUE
======================================================================

A manufacturer may seek rights to deploy PITN internally and commercialize the architecture for other manufacturers.

This creates a separate commercial opportunity from the operating cost-savings analysis.

Three principal transaction structures are available.

OPTION A — OUTRIGHT ACQUISITION

Acquisition of specifically identified transferable intellectual property, software, documentation, domains, namespace-related assets, and other expressly included assets.

Ownership and transferability must be verified individually.

OPTION B — EXCLUSIVE MASTER COMMERCIALIZATION LICENSE

The original owner retains ownership but grants exclusive commercialization rights within a defined field, territory, and term.

The agreement must expressly authorize downstream sublicensing.

OPTION C — NON-EXCLUSIVE MASTER COMMERCIALIZATION LICENSE

The original owner retains ownership and may grant commercial rights to multiple partners.

The licensee receives defined internal-use and downstream commercialization rights.

12. ECONOMIC AND COMMERCIAL CONCLUSIONS
======================================================================

The financial models in this document establish several distinct economic considerations for robotics manufacturers evaluating PITN.

REGISTRATION ACQUISITION

The PITN-reported historical average purchase price of approximately $1.75 provides an illustrative registration acquisition assumption.

A qualifying 50% owner return produces a $0.875 consolidated net economic cost for an eligible namespace owner.

It does not automatically reduce the manufacturer's purchase expenditure.

REGISTRATION SCALE

Registration quantity is a material cost variable.

The manufacturer should determine which product definitions, revisions, physical instances, assemblies, and subsystems actually require registered identities.

MANUFACTURING SOFTWARE

The illustrative $257,000 annual software expenditure establishes a comparison point.

PITN is complementary infrastructure and does not automatically replace or reduce these software expenditures.

INFORMATION-WORK SAVINGS

The separate task-level model illustrates $57,350 in potential annual gross labor savings.

The larger hypothetical information-work scenario illustrates $572,500 in potential annual gross savings against a $2.5 million baseline.

Neither figure is a verified manufacturer result.

DEPLOYMENT ECONOMICS

Registration costs alone do not represent total adoption cost.

Implementation, licensing, integration, data preparation, security, training, network expenses, support, renewals, and internal labor must be included.

COMMERCIAL VALUE

A manufacturer with properly granted master commercialization rights may develop a downstream licensing business.

Outright acquisition and master commercialization licensing represent separate transaction structures requiring documented ownership, transferability, and sublicensing authority.

The economic case must ultimately be established through manufacturer-specific baseline measurements, complete deployment costs, verified commercial terms, and actual operating results.

The central financial proposition is that reducing the recurring cost of identifying, reconciling, retrieving, and reusing robotics information may create economic value beyond the initial acquisition cost of persistent component identities.

The value is not established by registration alone.

It is established by the measurable improvement of the information workflows that depend on those identities.
