# MANUFACTURER NAMESPACE ECONOMICS, COMMERCIAL PARTICIPATION, PERSISTENT IDENTITY AND BLOCKCHAIN EVIDENCE

## 1. A broader manufacturer economic opportunity

PITN's blockchain/Web3 namespace architecture introduces an economic and operational opportunity that extends beyond the registration of individual component identities.

A participating humanoid robotics manufacturer may be able to establish control of a designated component namespace, define registration pricing under the applicable commercial arrangement, participate in qualifying registration revenue, and develop a persistent identity registry for its component ecosystem.

This creates an opportunity to connect three economic and operational functions:

1. **Identity infrastructure:** Establish persistent identity references for physical components, product definitions, assemblies, and applicable physical instances.
2. **Commercial participation:** Establish namespace pricing and participate in qualifying registration revenue under the applicable ownership and revenue-sharing terms.
3. **Transaction and registration evidence:** Maintain operational registration records and associate them with blockchain mint transaction evidence where implemented.

These functions are complementary. They should not be confused with one another or treated as a single financial transaction.

The manufacturer's broader opportunity is to participate in an infrastructure model that can support component identification, information continuity, commercial registration, and downstream robotics ecosystem activity.

## 2. Namespace control as a manufacturer commercial asset

A namespace provides a designated identity category through which component registrations can be organized.

For example, a manufacturer participating in the PITN architecture may control a component namespace such as `.actuator`, subject to the applicable ownership, registration, and provider arrangements.

The manufacturer can establish a listed registration price within the permitted commercial structure.

This provides a potential mechanism for the manufacturer to establish a consistent identity-registration environment for its component ecosystem.

The economic significance is not simply that a manufacturer can register an actuator identity. It is that a designated namespace can become a reusable registration environment for multiple component identities and potentially multiple participants.

A namespace may support:

- Manufacturer component identities.
- Product-family registrations.
- Component model references.
- Supplier participation.
- Authorized registration activity.
- Persistent identity references.
- Registry expansion as product lines grow.
- Commercial registration activity under applicable terms.

Namespace control, legal ownership, technical administration, and revenue entitlement must be distinguished. The exact rights depend on the governing agreements and provider arrangements. A namespace registration should not be represented as transferring ownership of a physical component, its intellectual property, or its manufacturer's trademarks.

## 3. Manufacturer-defined registration pricing

The accompanying PITN infographic illustrates a manufacturer-controlled pricing model using a listed registration price of $5.00.

The manufacturer establishes the listed price for registrations within its designated namespace, subject to applicable platform and provider terms.

Promotional discounts may be available during qualifying periods.

This allows the manufacturer to evaluate different registration price points and promotional strategies while maintaining a defined registration value.

The illustrative example uses:

| Pricing element | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Listed registration price | $5.00 |
| Promotional discount | 50% |
| Actual purchaser payment | $2.50 |

The promotional price is the amount paid by the purchaser in this example.

A promotional discount is not itself a revenue share. It changes the actual purchase price upon which the illustrative revenue-share calculation is based.

## 4. The 50% namespace-owner revenue-share mechanism

The infographic illustrates a 50% share of the actual discounted purchase price being returned to the eligible namespace owner.

Using the example:

- Listed registration price: $5.00.
- Promotional discount: 50%.
- Actual purchase price: $2.50.
- Illustrative owner revenue share: 50% of $2.50.
- Owner revenue: $1.25.

The calculation is:

**$2.50 × 50% = $1.25 owner revenue.**

Where the manufacturer is both the purchaser and the eligible namespace owner receiving the share, the consolidated economics are:

**$2.50 purchase payment − $1.25 owner revenue = $1.25 net economic cost.**

This distinction is essential.

The purchaser pays $2.50. The $1.25 is a separate revenue receipt to the eligible owner. It is not a reduction in the checkout price.

If the purchaser and namespace owner are different parties, their economics must be calculated separately.

The applicable provider agreement must establish who qualifies as the owner, how revenue is calculated, when it is payable, whether fees or deductions apply, and what conditions govern eligibility.

## 6. Registry development at scale

The commercial model becomes more significant when considered across a manufacturer's component identity registry.

A humanoid robotics manufacturer may need identity references for actuators, reducers, encoders, bearings, sensors, controllers, power components, electrical connections, fasteners, assemblies, and other physical elements.

The number of registrations will depend on the identity policy.

A manufacturer must determine whether it registers:

- Product definitions.
- Individual product models.
- Distinct engineering revisions.
- Individual serialized physical units.
- Lots or production batches.
- Assemblies and subsystems.
- Complete robot configurations.

These are different identity levels. They should not be counted interchangeably.

The following example assumes every registration is acquired at the illustrative $2.50 promotional price and generates an eligible 50% owner share.

| Registration volume | Gross purchaser payments | Illustrative owner revenue | Consolidated net cost if same party is eligible owner |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1,000 | $2,500 | $1,250 | $1,250 |
| 5,000 | $12,500 | $6,250 | $6,250 |
| 10,000 | $25,000 | $12,500 | $12,500 |
| 25,000 | $62,500 | $31,250 | $31,250 |
| 50,000 | $125,000 | $62,500 | $62,500 |
| 100,000 | $250,000 | $125,000 | $125,000 |
| 250,000 | $625,000 | $312,500 | $312,500 |
| 500,000 | $1,250,000 | $625,000 | $625,000 |
| 1,000,000 | $2,500,000 | $1,250,000 | $1,250,000 |

These are registration-volume scenarios, not forecasts of manufacturer demand or guaranteed revenue.

They exclude transaction fees, taxes, operating costs, licensing, integration, support, and other contractual deductions.

The manufacturer must also avoid unnecessary registration duplication. A coherent identity policy should establish which objects require persistent identities and which should remain attributes or relationships of an existing identity.

## 7. The registry as an expanding information asset

The value of a component registry is not determined only by the number of registrations.

Its usefulness depends on the quality, authority, relationships, and continuing relevance of the information associated with those identities.

A manufacturer-controlled namespace can provide a structured entry point into a component information environment.

A registered identity may be associated with:

- Original manufacturer part numbers.
- Supplier SKUs.
- Product descriptions.
- Product models and revisions.
- Technical specifications.
- Mechanical and electrical interfaces.
- Datasheets and drawings.
- BOM positions.
- Parent assemblies.
- Supplier references.
- Quality documentation.
- Lot and serial references.
- Installation records.
- Maintenance and service history.
- Replacement and successor references.
- Recovery and disposition information.

The identity does not automatically contain or verify all of this information. The manufacturer must define the records, authoritative sources, permissions, and relationships that are appropriate to its operations.

The commercial opportunity is to establish a persistent identity framework through which this information can be organized and reused.

## 8. Two complementary evidence systems: registration records and blockchain transactions

The infographic identifies two distinct records.

They should be understood as complementary evidence sources.

### 8.1 Operational registration record

The dashboard record represents the registration information maintained by the registration platform.

The illustrated fields include:

- Registration name.
- Namespace or TLD.
- Registration status.
- Ownership information.
- Pricing information.

This record answers:

**What identity is registered, under which namespace, and what does the registration platform report about its status and ownership?**

The registration dashboard is an operational information source. Its contents depend on the platform's implementation and data-maintenance practices.

### 8.2 Blockchain mint transaction record

The blockchain mint transaction provides transaction-level evidence associated with the mint operation.

The infographic identifies:

- Transaction hash.
- Timestamp.
- Block number, where available.
- Mint input data.

This record answers:

**What transaction was submitted to the blockchain, and what transaction evidence can be independently inspected on the applicable network?**

A transaction hash provides a reference to a blockchain transaction. A timestamp and block number provide additional context, subject to the network and available transaction data.

Mint input data may identify information submitted as part of the transaction, depending on the implementation and encoding.

### 8.3 Linking the two records

Where the implementation supports it, the operational registration record should retain a reference to its associated blockchain transaction.

This relationship can make it possible to move from a registered identity record to the relevant transaction evidence.

The two records should remain distinguishable.

A dashboard may report a registration as active, while the blockchain evidence establishes a particular mint transaction. Neither record automatically validates every attribute of the other.

A transaction hash alone does not prove that the physical component is authentic, that the manufacturer supplied it, or that its technical specifications are correct.

## 9. Physical identity, registration evidence, and real-world verification

A central principle of PITN is the distinction between a persistent digital identity and the physical object to which that identity refers.

The registration process establishes an identity reference under the applicable namespace and registration rules.

The blockchain transaction can provide evidence that a mint transaction was recorded.

The physical-world association requires additional evidence and controls.

For example, a manufacturer may need to establish:

- Who created the component identity.
- Which organization is authoritative for the product definition.
- Which physical component was assigned the identity.
- How the identity is applied or attached to the component.
- Whether the physical identifier can be read or inspected.
- How the component is associated with its lot or serial number.
- How installation into a robot is recorded.
- How changes, replacements, and lifecycle events are maintained.

These processes are essential if the manufacturer intends to use identity references for quality, service, warranty, recall, or physical traceability.

Blockchain immutability does not independently guarantee the truth of information submitted to the blockchain.

The integrity of the broader identity system depends on source authority, assignment procedures, physical verification, access controls, and governance.

## 10. Commercial participation beyond the manufacturer's own registrations

A manufacturer-controlled namespace may have potential commercial relevance beyond the manufacturer's internal identity requirements.

Depending on the rights and participation model, a manufacturer could evaluate registration activity involving:

- Its own component product families.
- Authorized distributors.
- Contract manufacturers.
- Approved suppliers.
- System integrators.
- Aftermarket participants.
- Service organizations.
- Other organizations participating in the component ecosystem.

This creates the possibility of a wider registration environment around a manufacturer's component category.

For example, a manufacturer with a designated actuator namespace could evaluate how component identity registrations are made available to relevant participants and how those registrations relate to the manufacturer's authoritative product information.

This must be governed by clear rules covering registration eligibility, identity creation, data authority, intellectual property, access, commercial rights, and dispute resolution.

The namespace should not be presented as proof that every third-party registration is manufacturer-authorized unless an authorization process actually exists.

## 11. Potential downstream commercial applications

Persistent component identity can support additional commercial and operational activities when implemented with the necessary systems and governance.

Potential applications include:

**Supplier participation:** Suppliers can associate authorized product and supplier references with established component identities.

**Engineering collaboration:** Engineering teams can reuse component references and applicable product documentation across programs.

**Procurement and sourcing:** Product identities can help connect manufacturer part numbers, supplier SKUs, and sourcing information.

**Aftermarket and replacement:** Service organizations can use persistent references to locate product definitions and replacement information.

**Maintenance and repair:** Physical-unit identities can be related to installation and service records.

**Quality and traceability:** Product and physical-unit references can be associated with applicable quality evidence and lifecycle information.

**Recovery and refurbishment:** Component identity can provide continuity between original product information, service history, and recovery records.

**Intelligent robotics applications:** Persistent physical identity can provide a reference foundation for downstream robotics software and agent systems that require authorized component and configuration context.

These are potential applications, not automatic features of namespace ownership or minting. Each requires appropriate technical implementation and commercial authorization.

## 12. Relationship to the PITN information-cost reduction model

The namespace and revenue-share model must remain separate from the operational information-cost model.

PITN's illustrative $2.5 million annual information-cost scenario and modeled $572,500 annual gross savings address potential reductions in recurring information work.

The namespace model addresses registration acquisition economics and potential revenue participation.

The blockchain transaction record addresses evidence associated with registration minting.

These categories should be separately calculated:

| Economic or operational category | What it measures |
|---|---|
| Information-cost savings | Reduction in recurring information-related labor and operational costs |
| Registration acquisition cost | Amount paid to register component identities |
| Namespace-owner revenue | Eligible revenue share received under applicable terms |
| Registry operating costs | Costs to administer and maintain the registration environment |
| Implementation costs | Data preparation, integration, configuration, testing, and deployment |
| Licensing and support | Recurring commercial and technical costs |
| Blockchain/network costs | Applicable transaction and network expenses |
| Downstream commercial revenue | Revenue from separately authorized licensing or services |

The manufacturer should consolidate these categories only after each has been independently established.

Registration revenue must not be represented as operating-cost savings. A modeled reduction in engineering labor must not be counted again as a separate procurement or data-cleanup benefit if the same hours are already included.

## 13. Manufacturer investment and adoption considerations

A manufacturer evaluating namespace participation should assess:

1. The rights and responsibilities associated with namespace control.
2. Registration pricing authority and permitted promotional arrangements.
3. Revenue-share eligibility and payout conditions.
4. The identity policy for products, revisions, assemblies, lots, and physical units.
5. The relationship between dashboard records and blockchain transaction evidence.
6. The authoritative systems and organizations responsible for product information.
7. The technical process for identity creation and assignment.
8. The integration required with existing enterprise systems.
9. Security, permissions, and confidential-data controls.
10. Registry administration and ongoing maintenance.
11. The complete cost of implementation and operation.
12. The ability to demonstrate measurable manufacturer benefits.

A manufacturer should not evaluate the model solely by multiplying a registration price by the number of components in a hypothetical robot.

The appropriate registration count depends on the identity policy, the actual product structure, the intended traceability level, and the commercial use of the namespace.

## 14. Commercial conclusion

PITN's namespace model creates a potential commercial participation layer around persistent component identity.

A participating manufacturer may be able to control a designated component namespace, establish registration pricing under the applicable arrangements, take advantage of qualifying promotional pricing, and receive a contractual share of eligible registration revenue.

The resulting registrations can form part of a persistent component identity environment.

Operational dashboard records provide registration information. Blockchain mint transactions provide separate transaction-level evidence. When the implementation links these records, they can provide complementary visibility into registration status and the transaction associated with minting.

The broader opportunity is the development of a reusable identity and information environment for a manufacturer's component ecosystem.

This opportunity complements PITN's primary information-cost proposition: reducing repeated information discovery, reconciliation, and reconstruction by connecting physical components to the information that already describes them.

The manufacturer is therefore evaluating more than a registration price.

It is evaluating potential participation in an identity infrastructure that can connect component information, support lifecycle continuity, provide transaction evidence, and create additional commercial opportunities under defined rights and operating arrangements.

*All registration prices, promotional discounts, revenue-share amounts, and scaled scenarios in this section are illustrative. Actual namespace rights, pricing authority, eligibility, revenue calculations, fees, payouts, technical implementation, and transaction behavior are governed by the applicable provider and commercial agreements. No physical authenticity, product certification, or manufacturer authorization is established solely by registration or blockchain minting.*
