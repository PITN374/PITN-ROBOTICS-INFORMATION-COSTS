# A Manufacturer’s Guide to the POWER IN THE NUMBERS 34-Namespace Robotics Identity Architecture

## From Blockchain-Based Namespace Infrastructure to Component Identity, Registration Economics, and Operational Integration

### Purpose

POWER IN THE NUMBERS (PITN) has developed a 34-namespace robotics identity architecture designed to provide manufacturers with a structured way to connect physical robotic components to the information that already describes them.

The framework addresses a persistent information problem in robotics: components, assemblies, suppliers, technical records, revisions, and lifecycle information are frequently identified across separate systems using identifiers that are difficult to connect and maintain consistently.

PITN’s central principle is:

**CONNECT THE PHYSICAL COMPONENT TO THE INFORMATION THAT ALREADY DESCRIBES IT.**

The architecture uses blockchain-based namespace infrastructure to establish category-specific digital naming structures for robotics components and systems. It is intended to complement existing ERP, PLM, MES, CAD, PDM, QMS, and manufacturing information systems—not replace them.

This document explains the framework from a manufacturer’s operational perspective. It covers namespace structure, component registration, dashboard administration, illustrative pricing economics, reseller functionality, and blockchain transaction evidence.

## 1. What the Manufacturer Is Acquiring

PITN reports that it has acquired and minted 34 blockchain-based namespace assets organized around robotics systems, assemblies, components, sensing, control, power infrastructure, vision, and tooling.

Together, these namespaces form a classification architecture for identifying robotic equipment and its constituent parts.

The architecture is designed to support a manufacturer that needs to:

- Establish consistent digital names for components and assemblies.
- Associate component names with manufacturer part numbers and internal SKUs.
- Connect component identities to bills of materials and assembly structures.
- Reference technical specifications, drawings, certifications, and supporting documents.
- Maintain relationships among manufacturers, suppliers, components, and finished systems.
- Track revisions, replacements, service history, and lifecycle status.
- Provide a consistent reference structure across engineering, procurement, production, service, and supply-chain operations.

The 34 namespaces are organized into eight functional areas:

## 2. Understand the Three Layers of Identity

The framework separates three related but distinct elements.

### Layer 1 — The namespace

A namespace is the category-specific digital naming structure.

For example, an actuator-related namespace provides a dedicated naming category for actuator products and assemblies.

The namespace establishes the classification environment. It does not, by itself, identify or authenticate an individual physical component.

### Layer 2 — The registered digital name

A manufacturer can register an available name beneath the appropriate namespace.

**Illustrative example only:**

`ACT-EXAMPLE.actuator`

This is a fictional example, not an actual PITN registration.

The registered name provides a persistent digital reference that can be associated with a manufacturer's product or component records.

### Layer 3 — The component information record

The manufacturer connects the registered name to the information describing the physical component.

A component record may include:

- Manufacturer and supplier.
- Manufacturer part number (MPN).
- Stock keeping unit (SKU).
- Product model and revision.
- Serial number or production lot.
- Bill-of-materials position.
- Technical specifications.
- Drawings and supporting documentation.
- Compatibility and interface information.
- Maintenance and service records.
- Lifecycle and replacement status.

The registered name is a reference to the record. It does not automatically create, verify, or publish the underlying component information.

Likewise, a blockchain registration does not independently prove that a physical component is genuine, correctly manufactured, certified, or safe.

## 3. How a Manufacturer Operates the Architecture

The following workflow describes how a manufacturer can put the architecture into operation.

### Step 1 — Classify the component

Determine which namespace category best represents the component or assembly.

For example, a motion-producing device would be associated with the relevant actuator category rather than being assigned an unrelated general-purpose identity.

Classification should follow the manufacturer's product taxonomy and documented naming rules.

### Step 2 — Establish a naming convention

Define a consistent naming format for registered component identities.

A manufacturer may choose conventions based on product family, model, component type, or internal part-number structure.

**Illustrative example only:**

- Component reference: `ACT-EXAMPLE`
- Digital name: `ACT-EXAMPLE.actuator`
- Internal manufacturer part number: `MPN-EXAMPLE-001`

These sample identifiers are fictional and do not represent recorded PITN or manufacturer data.

The naming convention should be documented before registrations begin to avoid duplicate, ambiguous, or inconsistent identities.

### Step 3 — Register the digital name

The manufacturer or authorized operator uses the applicable registration interface to search for and register an available name beneath the relevant namespace.

The registration provider controls the registration process, availability, applicable charges, and registration conditions.

A successful registration creates the digital name under the provider's applicable terms. It does not automatically populate the manufacturer's product database or connect the name to a physical component.

### Step 4 — Configure pricing where permitted

Where the registration platform provides owner-controlled pricing or reseller settings, the authorized namespace operator can configure available commercial options within the provider's current rules.

Pricing eligibility, permitted price ranges, name-length conditions, and other restrictions are determined by the registration provider.

PITN does not control provider pricing policies and cannot guarantee that a particular price or pricing option will remain available.

### Step 5 — Connect the name to the component record

The manufacturer associates the registered name with the appropriate internal component record.

This can be performed through an existing product-information system, an integration layer, or a separately maintained identity database.

The connection should preserve the manufacturer's authoritative records and identify the source of each important data field.

### Step 6 — Connect the component to the bill of materials

The digital identity can then be associated with its position within an assembly or complete robotic system.

For example, a component record may link to:

- The assembly in which it is installed.
- The parent product or robotic system.
- Other components connected to it.
- Its manufacturer and supplier.
- Its applicable revisions and technical documentation.

This creates a structured relationship between the component, its surrounding assembly, and the information already maintained by the manufacturer.

### Step 7 — Maintain the identity over the component lifecycle

The manufacturer maintains the associated record as the product changes.

Relevant events may include:

- Engineering revisions.
- Supplier changes.
- Production and lot updates.
- Installation and commissioning.
- Maintenance and repair.
- Component replacement.
- Refurbishment and reuse.
- Retirement or end-of-life disposition.

The identity framework provides a consistent reference point. The manufacturer remains responsible for the accuracy, authorization, and maintenance of its own records.

## 4. Dashboard Administration

The registration dashboard provides an administrative view of namespace assets and the settings available for each one.

Depending on the provider's current functionality, an operator may be able to review:

- Namespace status.
- Registered-name counts.
- Available registration options.
- Royalty or revenue-participation settings.
- Reseller functionality.
- Registration activity.
- Earned, available, or claimable balances.
- Applicable transaction and account information.

These are separate operational indicators.

For example, a namespace may have reseller functionality enabled while its royalty setting is inactive. Enabling a reseller page does not automatically activate revenue participation.

Similarly, an active royalty setting does not mean that revenue has been generated. Revenue depends on qualifying transactions under the provider's applicable terms.

A manufacturer should review each namespace individually rather than assume that all settings are identical across the portfolio.

### Recommended operating practice

Maintain an internal namespace register containing:

1. Namespace category.
2. Authorized owner or operator.
3. Registration and administrative status.
4. Royalty or revenue-participation status.
5. Reseller status.
6. Registered-name count.
7. Relevant transaction references.
8. Responsible administrator.
9. Last review date.

This internal register should be maintained separately from public-facing documentation.

## 5. Reseller Functionality and Revenue Participation

Where supported by the registration provider, a namespace owner may enable a reseller page through which third parties can search for and register available names beneath that namespace.

This can create an additional registration channel for manufacturers, suppliers, integrators, distributors, and other participants in the robotics ecosystem.

A reseller page may provide a way for external parties to register relevant names without requiring the namespace owner to process every registration individually.

However, three distinctions are important:

**Reseller activation is not a sale.** An active reseller page does not guarantee registrations, customers, or revenue.

**Revenue participation is a separate setting.** The applicable royalty or revenue-sharing configuration must be reviewed independently for each namespace.

**Payment is subject to provider terms.** Eligibility, calculation, deductions, payment processing, and availability of funds are determined by the provider's current rules.

The framework therefore creates a potential channel for third-party registration activity, not a guaranteed source of income.

## 7. Promotional Pricing and Provider-Controlled Conditions

Promotional offers may reduce the amount paid by a customer for an eligible registration.

The availability and conditions of such offers are controlled by the registration provider and may depend on factors such as:

- Promotional periods.
- Eligible name categories.
- Name length.
- Quantity thresholds.
- Account or registration conditions.
- Changes to provider policies.

PITN cannot guarantee the continuation, availability, or terms of a promotion.

Where a 50% owner share applies to a discounted qualifying purchase, the share is calculated using the actual discounted purchase price.

Consequently, a lower promotional price may improve customer adoption while also reducing the corresponding owner revenue participation per registration.

Manufacturers should evaluate promotional pricing as a commercial option, not as a fixed or permanent acquisition-cost assumption.

## 8. Registration Duration and Lifetime-Registration Provisions

Some eligible domain registrations may be offered under a provider's lifetime-registration provision.

Where the provider's current terms expressly apply, this may remove recurring domain renewal charges for the eligible registered name.

The scope of that provision must be understood correctly.

There are distinct items:

1. **Namespace asset:** The underlying category-specific namespace.
2. **Registered domain name:** An individual name registered beneath that namespace.
3. **Optional services:** Additional provider features, account services, integrations, hosting, or other offerings.

A lifetime-registration provision for an eligible domain name must not be assumed to cover the namespace asset itself or every optional service.

It also does not eliminate blockchain network transaction fees, hosting expenses, data storage, software integration, maintenance, or other operational costs.

The manufacturer should verify the registration duration, renewal conditions, and applicable terms for each asset and service before making long-term cost assumptions.

The framework is intended to work alongside the systems manufacturers already use.

A practical integration may connect a registered digital name to:

| Existing information system | Potential connection |
|---|---|

| PLM | Product definitions, engineering revisions, and lifecycle records |
| MES | Production orders, manufacturing execution, and process records |

| QMS | Quality records, inspection results, and corrective actions |

The namespace provides a consistent digital reference. The manufacturer's existing systems remain the authoritative sources for the information they manage.

Integration can be implemented through APIs, data mappings, middleware, or other technical interfaces selected by the manufacturer.

The appropriate implementation depends on the manufacturer's existing architecture, data permissions, security requirements, and operational environment.

Registration alone does not create an integration. The manufacturer or its implementation partner must build, configure, test, and maintain the connections.

## 11. Data Governance, Security, and Verification

A manufacturer deploying the architecture should establish clear rules for the information associated with each identity.

Recommended controls include:

- Assigning authorized administrators.
- Defining who can create or modify component records.
- Preserving source-system references.
- Maintaining revision and change history.
- Separating public information from commercially sensitive records.
- Applying access controls to technical documents.
- Avoiding publication of confidential supplier, customer, production, or security information.
- Maintaining backups and recovery procedures.
- Verifying blockchain transaction evidence independently.
- Establishing procedures for disputed, superseded, or retired component identities.

The namespace is an identification and reference structure, not a substitute for cybersecurity controls, product testing, regulatory compliance, or engineering validation.

In particular, classification under a safety-related namespace does not certify a product or establish that a safety function meets any particular standard. Such claims require independent technical evidence, applicable conformity assessment, and manufacturer documentation.

Successful operation requires a clear separation of responsibilities.

PITN developed the 34-namespace robotics identity architecture and its intended classification structure.

Its role is to define the framework and explain how the namespace infrastructure can support persistent digital references for robotics components and systems.

- Registering available names.

### Blockchain network

The selected blockchain network processes and records transactions according to its own protocol.

PITN and the manufacturer do not control the network's underlying operation, transaction processing, or protocol rules.

## 13. Operational Sequence: From Namespace to Physical Component

The complete operating concept can be summarized as follows:

1. Select the appropriate namespace category.
2. Establish the manufacturer's naming convention.
3. Register an available digital name under the relevant namespace.
4. Review applicable registration terms and pricing.
5. Configure eligible reseller and revenue-participation settings where desired.
6. Retain the registration and blockchain transaction evidence.
7. Create or identify the corresponding manufacturer component record.
8. Associate the digital name with the MPN, SKU, serial or lot information, and product revision.
9. Connect the component to its BOM position and parent assembly.
10. Link the manufacturer's technical and commercial records.
11. Integrate the identity reference with existing enterprise systems.
12. Maintain the record throughout production, service, replacement, and end-of-life activity.

This sequence connects the digital naming layer to the manufacturer's existing product-information environment.

## Conclusion

The POWER IN THE NUMBERS 34-namespace robotics identity architecture provides a category-based blockchain naming foundation for connecting robotic systems, assemblies, and components to the information that already describes them.

Its practical value is the ability to establish consistent digital references across fragmented product and manufacturing information environments.

For a manufacturer, the operating concept is straightforward:

**Classify the component. Register its digital name. Connect that name to the manufacturer's authoritative records. Maintain the relationship throughout the component lifecycle.**

The architecture does not require a manufacturer to abandon its existing ERP, PLM, MES, CAD, PDM, or quality systems. It provides an additional identity and reference layer that can be integrated with those systems.

PITN's 34 namespaces establish the underlying robotics classification architecture. The manufacturer determines how to apply that architecture to its products, data, engineering workflows, supply chain, and commercial operations.

The objective is to make component identity more consistent, information relationships easier to maintain, and the connection between physical robotics hardware and its digital information more persistent.
