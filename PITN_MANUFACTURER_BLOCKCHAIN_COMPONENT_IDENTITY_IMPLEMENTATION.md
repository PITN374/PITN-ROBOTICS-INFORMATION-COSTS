## 1. Abstract

POWER IN THE NUMBERS (PITN) establishes a blockchain-based namespace architecture for connecting physical robotics components to persistent digital identities and the information associated with them.

The framework uses 34 robotics-related namespaces to organize component identification across robot systems, mechanical assemblies, actuators, reducers, sensors, controllers, power systems, vision modules, and tooling.

A manufacturer can register a namespace, establish its own registration pricing where permitted, associate registered names with its component catalog, and connect those identities to manufacturer-controlled product information, bills of materials (BOMs), specifications, suppliers, and lifecycle records.

Under the registration provider's current terms and conditions, the namespace owner receives 50% of the proceeds from qualifying domain purchases. The owner may also choose to activate Reseller Pages, creating an additional route through which other parties can register names under that namespace. This optional arrangement can allow a robotics manufacturer to establish a broader registration ecosystem and participate in revenue generated by registrations under its TLD.

The framework also incorporates lifetime domain registration under the provider's current terms, registration dashboard records, blockchain mint transaction evidence, and the connection of namespace identities to existing industrial information systems.

All registration economics, revenue participation, pricing permissions, promotional offers, reseller functionality, and lifetime registration provisions described here are subject to the registration provider's applicable terms and conditions. These policies are controlled by the provider, not PITN, and may change.

## 3. Manufacturer-Controlled Registration and Pricing

A manufacturer acquiring or controlling a namespace can establish a registration structure for its intended products and component identities.

Where permitted by the registration provider's current terms, the namespace owner can determine registration pricing. The current terms described for this framework permit pricing of up to $5.00 for qualifying names, depending on domain length and applicable provider conditions.

This creates an opportunity for manufacturers to establish a consistent registration model for their component categories.

For example, a manufacturer could:

1. Select a namespace relevant to its product category.
2. Establish a registration price within the provider's permitted pricing rules.
3. Register names corresponding to product models, component families, or other manufacturer-defined identities.
4. Associate each registered identity with its product records and technical information.
5. Use the registered identity as a persistent reference point across product documentation, BOMs, supplier records, and lifecycle information.

The manufacturer retains control of its own product information and the way it organizes those records. Registration does not automatically create, validate, or publish manufacturer data.

## 4. Registration Economics and Revenue Participation

Under the registration provider's current terms and conditions, the namespace owner receives 50% of qualifying purchase proceeds.

The 50% participation is calculated against the actual purchase price paid after any applicable promotional discount, not the original listed price.

### Illustrative pricing examples

Assume a manufacturer establishes a permitted listed registration price of $5.00.

| Pricing scenario | Customer purchase price | 50% owner share | Illustrative net cost to owner |

The net cost illustration assumes the namespace owner receives the stated 50% share and applies that amount against the purchase cost. It excludes any separate fees, taxes, transaction charges, or other costs that may apply.

- Average purchase price: $1.75

This historical average is a reported PITN figure, not an independently audited market price or a guarantee of future registration costs.

### Promotional pricing

The registration provider may make promotional discounts available. Promotions described in the current framework have ranged from approximately 40% to 90%, sometimes subject to minimum purchase quantities or other conditions.

Promotional availability, discount levels, eligibility, duration, and purchase requirements are determined by the provider. PITN does not control, guarantee, or independently authorize these offers.

The namespace owner's 50% share is calculated on the actual discounted purchase price under the current terms described above.

## 5. Lifetime Domain Registration

**Under the registration provider's current lifetime registration terms, a registered domain does not require recurring domain renewal fees. Once registered under those terms, the domain remains registered for its lifetime without ongoing domain renewal payments.**

For a manufacturer registering a large collection of component identities, this provision can materially affect the long-term economics of maintaining a namespace-based identity structure.

The distinction is important:

- No recurring domain renewal fee is required under the current lifetime registration provision.
- This statement concerns domain registration renewal only.
- It does not eliminate possible blockchain transaction fees, hosting expenses, data storage costs, integration expenses, or other third-party charges.
- Lifetime registration remains subject to the provider's applicable terms and conditions.

The registration provider controls these provisions. They are not independently guaranteed or administered by PITN, and they may be changed by the provider.

## 6. Optional Reseller Pages: An Additional Manufacturer Revenue Model

A namespace owner may also have the option to activate associated Reseller Pages, subject to the registration provider's current functionality, eligibility requirements, and terms.

This is an important additional capability because it allows a manufacturer to consider opening its namespace to registrations by other parties.

When Reseller Pages are activated, **other users and organizations can register available names under that TLD**, subject to the provider's registration rules and the namespace owner's applicable settings.

A robotics manufacturer could therefore establish two complementary uses for its namespace:

1. **Internal identity infrastructure:** Register names for its own product models, component families, assemblies, and related manufacturer records.
2. **External registration channel:** Make eligible names available to other parties through the activated Reseller Pages, creating an opportunity to participate in registration revenue generated under the namespace.

### Illustrative manufacturer reseller model

A manufacturer controlling a relevant robotics namespace could activate its Reseller Pages and make registrations available to:

- Component suppliers
- Robotics integrators
- System builders
- Robotics research organizations
- OEMs and contract manufacturers
- Other businesses and users operating within the relevant component category

For example, a manufacturer associated with actuator technology could make its namespace available to external registrants who want category-specific digital names for their own actuator products, component records, or related applications.

The manufacturer could thereby establish a namespace-centered commercial ecosystem rather than limiting the namespace to its own internal registrations.

Under the current 50% revenue participation terms described in this document, the namespace owner may receive a share of qualifying registration proceeds from purchases under its TLD. The applicable share is calculated using the actual purchase price after discounts.

This creates a potential additional revenue channel alongside the manufacturer's primary business of producing and selling robotics components.

### Important reseller qualifications

- Reseller Pages are optional; the namespace owner must choose to activate them where the provider makes that functionality available.
- External registration is subject to the provider's registration rules and the namespace owner's applicable settings.
- The namespace owner does not automatically receive revenue merely by owning a namespace; the relevant reseller functionality and registration activity must be in place.
- Revenue participation, payout procedures, eligible transactions, pricing controls, and other commercial conditions are governed by the provider's current terms.
- Promotional discounts and other pricing changes affect the actual purchase price on which the current 50% share is calculated.
- PITN does not control the provider's reseller platform, registration processing, payment systems, or terms.

The reseller model is therefore an optional commercial extension of the namespace architecture, not a guaranteed revenue stream.

## 7. Registration and Blockchain Minting Process

The registration process establishes the namespace identity and its associated transaction evidence.

A typical implementation sequence is:

1. **Namespace selection:** The manufacturer identifies the relevant PITN namespace.
2. **Registration name selection:** The manufacturer determines the name to register under the namespace.
3. **Pricing determination:** The manufacturer applies a permitted registration price, subject to the provider's current rules.
4. **Purchase and registration:** The registration is completed through the provider's available registration process.
5. **Dashboard record:** The registration appears in the provider's management interface, subject to the information displayed there.
6. **Blockchain minting:** The corresponding mint transaction is processed on the selected blockchain network.
7. **Transaction evidence:** The resulting transaction information can be retained as a reference for the registered identity.
8. **Manufacturer data association:** The manufacturer connects the registered identity to its own component and product information.

Where the provider supports the relevant functionality, the namespace owner may also activate Reseller Pages and make eligible names available for external registration.

Registration, dashboard management, minting, and manufacturer information association are related but distinct operations.

## 8. Registration Dashboard Records and Blockchain Evidence

Two different records are relevant to the registration process.

### A. Registration dashboard record

The registration provider's dashboard may display information such as:

- Registered domain name
- Namespace or TLD
- Registration status
- Owner or account association
- Wallet association
- Registration and management information
- Available reseller settings, where supported

The dashboard is the management record presented by the registration provider. The exact fields and functions depend on the provider's interface and current services.

### B. Blockchain mint transaction record

The blockchain transaction provides a separate record of the minting event.

Depending on the network and transaction data available, this may include:

- Transaction hash
- Transaction timestamp
- Block number
- Transaction input data
- Registration name or namespace association
- Transaction source record

The transaction hash provides a reference that can be used to locate the relevant transaction on the applicable blockchain network.

The dashboard record and blockchain transaction record should not be treated as interchangeable. The dashboard supports registration management; the blockchain transaction provides evidence of the corresponding on-chain event.

Neither record, by itself, proves that a physical component is genuine, that manufacturer information is accurate, or that a product is safe, certified, or compatible.

## 9. Connecting the Registered Identity to a Physical Component

Once a manufacturer has established a registered identity, it can associate that identity with the information describing the physical component.

An illustrative actuator record could contain:

| Information category | Example |
|---|---|
| Robot asset | ROBOT-001 |
| Product model | ACT-220 |
| Component instance | RP-CI-000001 |
| Manufacturer serial | SN-1001 |
| Component category | Actuator |
| Namespace | `.actuator` |
| Digital identity | Manufacturer-defined registered name |
| Manufacturer | Manufacturer-controlled record |
| Product specification | Manufacturer-defined specification |
| BOM position | Defined assembly location |

These identifiers serve different purposes:

- The robot asset identifies the larger robot or system.
- The product model identifies a manufacturer's product type.
- The component instance identifies a particular component record.
- The serial number identifies the manufacturer's serialized item.
- The namespace identity provides a persistent digital naming reference.

The registered name does not replace the manufacturer's MPN, SKU, serial number, or internal component identifier. It provides an additional identity reference that can connect those existing identifiers and records.

## 10. Connecting Technical, Commercial, and Supplier Information

The identity framework can associate a component with relevant information maintained by the manufacturer or its authorized information sources.

A structured component record may include:

1. Supplier
2. Part
3. Specification
4. Price
5. Minimum order quantity (MOQ)
6. Lead time
7. Location
8. Certification information
9. Alternatives
10. Compatibility
11. Availability

This structure allows the registered identity to serve as a reference point for information that may otherwise be distributed across supplier portals, product catalogs, engineering documents, and enterprise systems.

The manufacturer determines which records are associated with each component identity and which sources are authoritative for each data field.

Registration does not independently verify supplier statements, commercial terms, certification claims, or technical specifications. Those records remain subject to the manufacturer's validation and governance processes.

## 11. Bill of Materials and Assembly Relationships

PITN's component identity architecture can be applied at the individual component level and connected to larger assemblies.

For example:

**Robot asset → Assembly → Component model → Component instance → Registered identity → Technical and commercial records**

A robot manufacturer can associate individual component identities with their corresponding BOM positions and assembly relationships.

This can support:

- Component-level identification
- Assembly and subassembly relationships
- Manufacturer part number and SKU references
- Serial and lot-level traceability
- Revision and specification references
- Supplier and sourcing information
- Replacement and alternative component records
- Lifecycle and service documentation

The purpose is to connect the physical component to the information that already describes it.

## 13. Operational and Commercial Implications

The architecture presents several potential benefits for robotics manufacturers.

### Component identity

A namespace-based identity can provide a consistent digital reference for a component across product documentation, engineering records, and lifecycle information.

### Information connectivity

Associating component identities with BOMs, specifications, supplier records, and commercial information can reduce the effort required to locate and reconcile distributed records.

### Manufacturer-controlled namespace structure

Manufacturers can organize names around their component categories, product families, and identity requirements, subject to provider rules.

### Optional external registration

When Reseller Pages are activated, a manufacturer can open eligible registrations under its namespace to external parties, potentially creating a registration-based revenue channel.

### Registration economics

The current 50% revenue participation arrangement, available promotional pricing, and permitted owner pricing can affect the economics of acquiring and operating registrations.

### Lifetime registration

The provider's current lifetime registration provision removes recurring domain renewal fees for registrations covered by those terms.

These are architectural and commercial opportunities. Actual benefits depend on implementation, adoption, registration activity, provider policies, and the quality of the information associated with each identity.

## 16. Conclusion

POWER IN THE NUMBERS provides a 34-namespace blockchain-based architecture for connecting physical robotics components to persistent digital identities and the information that describes them.

A manufacturer can use the architecture to register component identities, associate them with product models and BOMs, and connect technical, supplier, commercial, and lifecycle records.

The current registration economics described here also present two distinct commercial opportunities: manufacturer registration of its own component identities and, where the owner chooses to activate Reseller Pages, the option to make eligible names available to external registrants under its namespace.

This second route allows a manufacturer to consider a broader namespace-based business model in which its TLD can serve both as an identity structure for its own products and as an optional registration platform for other participants.

Combined with the provider's current 50% revenue participation terms and lifetime domain registration provision, this creates a potential commercial extension to the underlying component identity framework.

The central objective remains:

**CONNECT THE PHYSICAL COMPONENT TO THE INFORMATION THAT ALREADY DESCRIBES IT.**
